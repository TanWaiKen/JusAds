# Localize Plan — Creative Strategy for Southeast Asian Markets

This document defines **how** the ad behaves creatively — its energy, pacing, and cultural hook
strategy. It is distinct from compliance (what is *allowed*) and platform style (where it runs).
The Director uses this plan to set scene energy, cut rhythm, and hook style before writing any
single scene.

---

## Available Creative Strategies

### 1. `meme_shock`
**"Stop the scroll with culture-native humor or absurdity"**

- Lead with a visual or line the audience immediately recognizes from local meme culture, viral moments, or shared Malaysian/SEA internet language.
- Humor is self-aware, fast, and never corporate. Think: reaction-face cuts, unexpected product appearances, dramatic audio stabs.
- Pacing: hyper-fast. 3–4 scenes in 10 seconds. No slow reveals. No cinematic breathing room.
- Text overlays are part of the joke — deadpan, ironic, or exaggerated.
- Scene transitions: hard cuts, zoom punches, or whip pans — never fades.
- Voiceover (if used): unscripted-feeling, like a friend talking, not an announcer.
- Works best for: F&B, e-commerce flash sales, youth brands, apps.

### 2. `culture_anchor`
**"Root the ad in a specific cultural moment, festival, or tradition"**

- Open with a visual the target community instantly owns — a festival greeting, a familiar setting (pasar malam, mamak, kopitiam), a generational value.
- Emotional warmth over shock. Build belonging, not surprise.
- Pacing: medium. 4–6 scenes in 15 seconds. Let the emotional beat breathe for 2–3 seconds.
- Language code-switching is encouraged (Manglish, mix of BM + English, etc.).
- Avoid generic "multicultural montage" — commit to one culture's perspective per ad.
- Works best for: Raya, CNY, Deepavali campaigns; family products; community-driven brands.

### 3. `problem_punchline`
**"State the problem fast, land the product as the punchline"**

- Hook = universally relatable Malaysian pain point (stuck in jam, makan too much, bill too high).
- Build just enough tension that the product reveal feels like relief.
- Pacing: fast. 4–5 scenes in 10–15 seconds. Problem scenes are quick; solution scene gets 2–3s.
- Subtitle tone: conversational, rhetorical ("You know this feeling?", "Betul ke?")
- Product reveal should feel satisfying — a sound cue or visual accent on the moment.
- Works best for: utility products, finance apps, health/wellness, food delivery.

### 4. `testimonial_burst`
**"Rapid social proof — reactions sell faster than features"**

- No single protagonist. Show multiple quick reactions: faces, before/afters, number reveals.
- Each scene is a different person or angle — creates the illusion of crowd validation.
- Pacing: rapid fire. 5–6 cuts in 10–15 seconds.
- Text overlays carry the story: short quotes, numbers, emojis as punctuation.
- Never invent testimonials — use this style only when the brief provides real product outcomes or user results.
- Works best for: supplements, services, skincare, marketplaces.

### 5. `speaker_led`
**"One person, one message — direct and confident"**

- Single on-screen personality speaks directly to camera. No cuts away.
- Energy: authoritative but approachable — neither corporate stiff nor overly casual.
- Works well with a TikTok-style hook opener then a hold on the speaker throughout.
- Pacing: deliberate. 3–4 scenes, each 3–5 seconds, allowing the speaker's words to land.
- Subtitles mandatory — the speaker's exact words appear on screen as they speak.
- Works best for: education, finance, health advice, brand ambassador content.

### 6. `product_hero`
**"No character — the product IS the star"**

- Pure product cinematography: macro shots, textures, pouring, unboxing, in-use beauty shots.
- No human face needed. Character is the product itself.
- Pacing: deliberate but not slow-motion. Crisp, clean, satisfying cuts on beats.
- Sound direction: ASMR-adjacent — sizzle, crunch, tap, swoosh — product Foley is the hook.
- Subtitles are minimal and precise — product name + single benefit.
- Works best for: food, beverages, skincare, tech gadgets, cleaning products.

---

## Pacing Reference (Mandatory)

These are the allowed scene densities. **Slow-motion sequences are prohibited** — they signal a generic TV commercial, not a native digital ad.

| Duration | Min scenes | Max scenes | Preferred cut rhythm |
|----------|-----------|-----------|---------------------|
| 10s      | 3         | 5         | Hard cut every 2–3s |
| 15s      | 4         | 6         | Hard cut every 2–3s |
| 20s      | 5         | 8         | Mix of 2s and 3s cuts |
| 30s      | 6         | 10        | Hard cuts + one 3–4s anchor scene |

> **Note:** The system supports 4 scenes = 10s and 6 scenes = 10s.
> Each scene gets a fixed duration slot — the Director must fill every slot with a purposeful
> visual beat, never pad with slow-motion or silent establishing shots.

---

## Anti-Patterns (never use regardless of strategy)

- ❌ Slow-motion product reveals that pad time without payoff
- ❌ Generic "golden hour lifestyle" montages with no product or hook
- ❌ Corporate-voice narration ("Introducing the revolutionary...")
- ❌ Fade-in brand logo as the opening scene
- ❌ Empty "mood" scenes with no text overlay and no visual action
- ❌ Cinematic establishing shots that burn 3+ seconds before anything happens
- ❌ Uniform pacing throughout — every high-performing ad has a rhythm change at the midpoint

---

## How the Director Uses This Plan

1. **Pick the hook style** from the strategy above — the opening scene must execute it.
2. **Set the cut rhythm** — never exceed 3s per scene unless it's a deliberate anchor beat.
3. **Sound direction per scene** must match the strategy energy:
   - `meme_shock` → hard stabs, bass drop, trending-beat energy
   - `culture_anchor` → melodic, warm, culturally-coded instrument cues
   - `problem_punchline` → tension-build, then satisfying resolution SFX
   - `testimonial_burst` → rapid notification/reaction sounds
   - `speaker_led` → minimal music, voice-forward, slight reverb warmth
   - `product_hero` → Foley-first, ASMR beats, minimal voiceover
4. **Never write a scene** that could exist in any generic global ad — every scene must be
   recognisably rooted in the target market's culture, language, and context.
